## Why this course exists

Data science graduates are usually taught to build models and rarely taught what happens next. In a first job, the question is seldom "can you improve the accuracy?" It is "which model is live, what data trained it, why did it change on Tuesday, and can you prove it?"

This course was written to close that gap for students who are studying alone, with no industry experience, no budget, and often a laptop running Windows. It assumes that operational competence, honesty about limitations, and care for the people affected by a system are part of the subject rather than an afterthought.

## What makes it different

- **Concept first, tool second.** Every tool is introduced after the problem it solves has been felt.
- **Windows-first, not Unix-by-accident.** PowerShell 7 is the primary shell, with labelled alternatives for Linux and macOS.
- **Free and local by default.** No paid service, credit card, or cloud account is required for any core outcome.
- **Honest about what is untested.** Every lab states whether it was verified and names any step that could not run here. Nothing is presented as working because it looks plausible.

## Credits and acknowledgements

This course teaches with other people's work, and says so:

- **Tools.** Python, Git, DVC, MLflow, scikit-learn, pandas, pandera, pytest, FastAPI, Docker, GitHub Actions, Prefect, Evidently, `kind`, Terraform, `gitleaks`, `pip-audit`, and `fairlearn` are maintained by their respective communities. Each is credited where it is used, and each remains under its own licence.
- **Ideas.** The treatment of hidden technical debt in machine learning follows the argument popularised by Sculley and colleagues in "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems" (NeurIPS, 2015). The distinction between reproducibility and replicability, and the framing of operational readiness, draw on the wider MLOps literature referenced in [Resources](../resources/index.md).
- **Data.** All datasets are synthetic or openly licensed. No personal data is used anywhere in the course.
- **Students.** The troubleshooting guide is built from problems students actually reported, which is why it names exact messages rather than general categories.

## Corrections and feedback

Corrections are welcome, especially reports that an instruction no longer works. Please include:

1. the page and the step number;
2. your operating system and tool version;
3. the exact message you saw, copied rather than summarised;
4. what you expected instead.

That is enough to reproduce almost any problem. Use the repository's issue tracker.

## Accessibility

The site aims to be usable with a keyboard, a screen reader, and at high zoom:

- headings follow a strict hierarchy, so the page is navigable by headings alone;
- every diagram and screenshot has alternative text describing its content, not its filename;
- link text describes its destination; "click here" is not used;
- colour never carries meaning on its own;
- code blocks are labelled with their language and can be copied without a mouse;
- no web fonts or tracking scripts are loaded, so pages work offline and render consistently.

If something is inaccessible, that is a defect. Please report it the same way you would report a broken command.
